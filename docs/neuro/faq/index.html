Update (2026-09-28). In this volume γ-band means the gamma rhythm, and DNA stiffness is written γDNA. A neuron’s rhythm is set by channel kinetics (τinh), not by γDNA. Working-memory capacity as the ratio fγ/fθ (gamma-band frequency over theta-band frequency, equivalently Tθ/Tγ) is graded [L]: it combines a literature identity (one item per gamma cycle nested in a theta cycle, Lisman & Idiart 1995; Lisman & Jensen 2013) with literature band edges. It is not derived from the R19 dynamics. The value depends on the band convention. The locked slow-gamma (25–50 Hz) and theta (4–8 Hz) edges give 6.25, and common conventions range from 4.3 to 11.6. The engine’s τ-pair gives 6.9, and the mind volume gives 6.1. The causal support is from the literature, cited as Vosskuhl, Strüber & Herrmann 2015 (Front. Hum. Neurosci. 9:257); Reinhart & Nguyen 2019 (Nat. Neurosci. 22:820). In those studies, slowing theta or targeting theta–gamma coupling changed span in the predicted direction, but the γ/θ ratio itself was not measured. That support is [L], not a VP-derived result. What is the one substrate the whole chain stands on?

The R19 bistable switch (ds/dt = g s − s³ + h), the jamming-lattice primitive that also writes DNA genes. A neuron is that switch plus a slow FitzHugh–Nagumo recovery — a low-frequency relaxation oscillator. Every cell in this volume runs the same switch; only the inherited measured γ changes.

Working-memory capacity equals the θ/γ ratio, about 7±2, because each gamma cycle nested in one theta cycle holds one item. It is the one link with causal validation: manipulating theta with tACS changes the realised capacity in the predicted direction (§4).
