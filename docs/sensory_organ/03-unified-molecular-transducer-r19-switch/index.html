The unified molecular transducer: every special sense is an R19 bistable channel switch

Every special-sense transducer is the same physical object: a cooperative or bistable ion channel — an R19 double-well switch. The photoreceptor (CNG), hair-cell (MET), and taste (TAS1R→TRPM5) channels are each modelled as bistable with a discontinuous flip (bistability holds by construction for any positive stiffness — a property of the model, not a verification); olfaction shares the rod's CNG superfamily. One switch primitive underlies five senses.

The transduction model tests each channel as an R19 double-well and finds bistability with a discontinuous flip and hysteresis for every transducer carrying a measured γ (by construction for any positive stiffness; the γ used are those of the master genes RAX, SOX2, TAS1R3, not of the channel genes). Sigmoid maximum slopes of 3.1–3.3 (cooperative gating) are a code output that depends on the sampling grid (21/41/81/161 points → 2.39/3.32/4.54/6.07; the slope diverges at the fold); the observed Hill coefficient ≈3 is an observation about the channel, not reproduced by the model. Channel identity and accession are cited [L]; γ for the effector genes is owned by the DNA pipeline (SSOT), entered as an honest to-measure input rather than invented.

The central result of the molecular layer is a unification: the transducers of vision, hearing, taste, and smell are not four mechanisms but four instances of one. Each is a cooperative or bistable ion channel, which is exactly the R19 double-well — two stable states separated by a barrier, with an all-or-none flip between them.

What makes a sensory channel a switch?

A transducer is a switch when it has two stable states and crosses between them sharply rather than gradually. Cast as an R19 double-well and driven, every channel with a vendored γ shows the three switch signatures: bistability (two stable conductances), a discontinuous flip, and hysteresis (the up- and down-thresholds differ). The hysteresis widths sit around 1.35–1.49. Cooperative gating makes the flip sharp

Maximum slopes of 3.1–3.3 are a code output on a 41-point grid and depend on it (21/41/81/161 points → 2.39/3.32/4.54/6.07; the slope diverges at the fold). The cooperative gating seen physiologically (Hill coefficient ≈3) is an observation about the channel (cited); the model does not reproduce it. Cooperativity is what converts a smooth change in stimulus into an all-or-none change in conductance, so each transducer behaves as a clean threshold detector rather than a graded one — the property that lets a single photon or a sub-nanometre bundle deflection produce a definite response.

Vision and smell share a literal common transducer

The olfactory channel CNGA2 belongs to the same cyclic-nucleotide-gated superfamily as the rod's CNGA1. Two senses therefore run on one switch primitive, with smell using cAMP where vision uses cGMP — the single most direct evidence that the special senses share a transducer, developed in phototransduction and chemodetection.
