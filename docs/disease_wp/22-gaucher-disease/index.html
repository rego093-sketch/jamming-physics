Gene, inheritance, and molecular mechanism

Gaucher disease is inherited as Autosomal recessive inheritance [L] and is classified mechanistically as loss-of-function (biallelic; recessive) [H]. Gaucher disease is glucocerebrosidase deficiency spanning a continuum from a perinatal-lethal form to an asymptomatic type. The causative gene is not annotated at this MedGen aggregate concept, so gene-level fields here are open and point to the gene-resolved type I section.

In-scope clinical involvement

The systemic (type 1) picture is hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias, and bone disease; the neuronopathic types 2 and 3 add a central-nervous-system course.

The OMIM clinical synopsis (the disease-level alternative) is API-key-gated and the key is unobtainable for an individual researcher — that path is removed, not guessed.

Established treatment and residual burden

The established disease-directed approach is enzyme replacement therapy (imiglucerase) with optional substrate reduction (eliglustat/miglustat) — non-neuronopathic type 1. Mechanistically: ERT replaces deficient glucocerebrosidase; substrate reduction lowers glucosylceramide synthesis [L].

Its effect on natural history is classified disease-modifying (substantial), mapping to an efficacy offset e = 0.5500 and a residual factor R_treat = 0.4500. Applied to the pre-treatment proxy this gives the residual burden score 0.2869, moving the disease from raw rank 18 to residual rank 21 (shift -3).

Evidence tier: accession-dated to the GeneReviews NBK1269 Management section [L] (initial posting July 27, 2000; last revision December 7, 2023; retrieved 2026-06-18; corroborating term(s): “enzyme, replacement, imiglucerase, optional, substrate, reduction, eliglustat, miglustat”). The natural-history axis grades remain a mix of [L] (GeneReviews-corroborated) and [H] (definition-only), so the burden order is still provisional.
