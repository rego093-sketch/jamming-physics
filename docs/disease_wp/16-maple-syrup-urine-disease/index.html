Maple syrup urine disease

Maple syrup urine disease is a metabolic genetic disease caused by autosomal recessive variants in BCKDHA, BCKDHB, acting through loss-of-function (biallelic; recessive). Within this volume's rankable burden cohort it sits at residual rank 15 of 23, where established therapy is substantially disease-modifying. Gene, inheritance, and molecular mechanism

Maple syrup urine disease is inherited as Autosomal recessive inheritance [L] and is classified mechanistically as loss-of-function (biallelic; recessive) [H]. The branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase complex (BCKDHA and BCKDHB subunits) decarboxylates the branched-chain amino acids. Its deficiency lets leucine and its ketoacid accumulate to neurotoxic levels.

In-scope clinical involvement

Classic maple syrup urine disease presents in the neonate with the characteristic maple-syrup odour, feeding difficulty, and encephalopathy, progressing without treatment to fatal cerebral oedema.

Established treatment and residual burden

The established disease-directed approach is branched-chain-amino-acid-restricted diet with thiamine in responsive individuals and a sick-day protocol. Mechanistically: dietary branched-chain amino-acid restriction prevents neurotoxic accumulation [L].

Its effect on natural history is classified disease-modifying (substantial), mapping to an efficacy offset e = 0.5500 and a residual factor R_treat = 0.4500. Applied to the pre-treatment proxy this gives the residual burden score 0.3375, moving the disease from raw rank 8 to residual rank 15 (shift -7).

Evidence tier: accession-dated to the GeneReviews NBK1319 Management section [L] (initial posting January 30, 2006; last revision April 23, 2020; retrieved 2026-06-18; corroborating term(s): “diet, thiamine”).
