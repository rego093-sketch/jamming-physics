Hurler syndrome

Hurler syndrome is a lysosomal genetic disease caused by autosomal recessive variants in IDUA, acting through loss-of-function (biallelic; recessive). Gene, inheritance, and molecular mechanism

Hurler syndrome is inherited as Autosomal recessive inheritance [L] and is classified mechanistically as loss-of-function (biallelic; recessive) [H]. IDUA encodes alpha-L-iduronidase. Its deficiency stores the glycosaminoglycans dermatan and heparan sulfate in lysosomes throughout the body (mucopolysaccharidosis type I), of which Hurler syndrome is the severe pole.

In-scope clinical involvement

Features include coarse facies, organomegaly, skeletal dysostosis multiplex, corneal clouding, hearing loss, and -- at the severe Hurler end -- progressive neurodegeneration.

Established treatment and residual burden

The established disease-directed approach is hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (early) with enzyme replacement (laronidase). Mechanistically: HSCT supplies enzyme-secreting donor cells (metabolic cross-correction); ERT replaces alpha-L-iduronidase somatically [L].

Its effect on natural history is classified disease-modifying (substantial), mapping to an efficacy offset e = 0.5500 and a residual factor R_treat = 0.4500. Applied to the pre-treatment proxy this gives the residual burden score 0.3375, moving the disease from raw rank 6 to residual rank 13 (shift -7).

Evidence tier: accession-dated to the GeneReviews NBK1162 Management section [L] (initial posting October 31, 2002; last revision December 4, 2025; retrieved 2026-06-18; corroborating term(s): “hematopoietic, stem, cell, transplantation, enzyme, replacement”). The natural-history axis grades remain a mix of [L] (GeneReviews-corroborated) and [H] (definition-only), so the burden order is still provisional.
