Tyrosinemia type II

Tyrosinemia type II is a metabolic genetic disease caused by autosomal recessive variants in TAT, acting through loss-of-function (biallelic; recessive). Gene, inheritance, and molecular mechanism

Tyrosinemia type II is inherited as Autosomal recessive inheritance [L] and is classified mechanistically as loss-of-function (biallelic; recessive) [H]. TAT encodes hepatic tyrosine aminotransferase, the first enzyme of tyrosine breakdown. Its deficiency raises plasma tyrosine, which crystallises in the cornea and skin.

In-scope clinical involvement

The disease is defined by corneal dystrophy, painful palmoplantar hyperkeratosis, and variable intellectual disability; individuals treated from early infancy may remain nearly asymptomatic.

Established treatment and residual burden

The established disease-directed approach is dietary tyrosine (and phenylalanine) restriction. Mechanistically: dietary tyrosine restriction prevents tyrosine crystal deposition in cornea and skin [L].

Its effect on natural history is classified disease-modifying (substantial), mapping to an efficacy offset e = 0.5500 and a residual factor R_treat = 0.4500. Applied to the pre-treatment proxy this gives the residual burden score 0.3000, moving the disease from raw rank 14 to residual rank 19 (shift -5).
