Hereditary factor IX deficiency disease

Hereditary factor IX deficiency disease is a hematologic genetic disease caused by X-linked recessive variants in F9, acting through haploinsufficiency. Within this volume's rankable burden cohort it sits at residual rank 20 of 23, where established therapy is substantially disease-modifying. Gene, inheritance, and molecular mechanism

Hereditary factor IX deficiency disease is inherited as X-linked recessive inheritance [L] and is classified mechanistically as haploinsufficiency [L]. F9 encodes coagulation factor IX. Deficient factor IX activity impairs the intrinsic coagulation cascade, so thrombin generation is reduced.

In-scope clinical involvement

This X-linked bleeding disorder causes haemarthroses, deep-tissue bleeds, and prolonged bleeding after trauma, dental work, or surgery, graded by the residual factor IX level.

Established treatment and residual burden

The established disease-directed approach is factor IX replacement or gene therapy (etranacogene dezaparvovec). Mechanistically: restores factor IX coagulant activity; AAV gene therapy provides endogenous factor IX [L].

Its effect on natural history is classified disease-modifying (substantial), mapping to an efficacy offset e = 0.5500 and a residual factor R_treat = 0.4500. Applied to the pre-treatment proxy this gives the residual burden score 0.2925, moving the disease from raw rank 16 to residual rank 20 (shift -4).
