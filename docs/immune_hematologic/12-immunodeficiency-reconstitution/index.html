Immunodeficiency reconstitution: one lever, two diseases

Acquired immunodeficiency is a collapse of a single surveillance-strength lever. Below a coverage threshold (measured surveillance 0.60) broad protection collapses and the pathogen burden rises as 1/surveillance, while on the SAME lever a tumour escapes below its own threshold (0.50) — one lever, two diseases (an opportunistic infection and an immune-escape malignancy), the unified substrate of the immunocompromised state. Reconstitution is THRESHOLD-GATED: restoring surveillance past a critical level (measured 0.50) re-establishes coverage and clears the burden, while sub-threshold restoration fails, and the required level scales with the DEPTH of the deficit. Partial immune reconstitution is not a partial cure — it must cross the coverage threshold.

Part of the disease/treatment axis: a pathological immune state is a latched basin, and the durable class of intervention moves the system across the saddle-node rather than only holding the drive down, which relapses on withdrawal.
