## 0. Verdict in five lines
1. **The volume never says it "solved the Navier–Stokes equations", and a careful reader would not come away believing it did.** It claims three things. (a) The *balance laws* (continuity and momentum) are exact identities of a particle arrangement [DERIVE]. (b) The *Newtonian closure* that turns them into Navier–Stokes holds only under conditions [GATE]. (c) *Global regularity* is **not** proved [GATE]. The numerical solvers are standard pseudo-spectral codes that check invariants. They do not solve the equations in any new sense. No turbulence closure is proposed. If the author describes the volume as "solved NS", that goes beyond the volume's own text.
2. The volume's own statements of *how strong* the claim is disagree with each other. The Newtonian closure is called "exact" (§14) and also "conditional [GATE]" (§1, §6). The number of open gates is given as "two", "lone" (one), "two frontiers" (a different pair), "all three" and a list of about seven.
3. Many checks marked PASS **cannot fail**, because they test a definition, a formula or Gauss's theorem. They sit next to real tests without any label telling them apart. A single reading reader will overrate the evidence.
4. Symbols are heavily overloaded (σ, ε, μ, λ, α, γ, d, Π). Key objects (Π_T, Π_ST, a, b, ψ, χ, "JFM exponent", "metriplectic" at first use) are used before they are defined or are never defined.
5. Rendering: raw LaTeX survives on 14 pages (`Section~\ref{sec:…}`, `\S§3`, `(eq.)`, `\emph{not}`, `\begin{verbatim}`, `\end{document}`, `$Pi$`). Two unescaped `<data…>` placeholders swallow text. 22 URL slugs contain `texttt`.

Hub completeness is **OK**. The hub lists all 38 child pages. The prev/next chain runs §1 → §14 → Appendix A → … → Appendix X in a straight line with no gaps. There are no stubs; the shortest page is Appendix F at about 190 words, which is adequate for a module note.

## Suggested one-paragraph statement for the hub and §1 (for R3-01, R3-02 and R3-03)
> **What this volume does with Navier–Stokes.** It *derives* the mass and momentum balance laws exactly, as bookkeeping identities of a coarse-grained particle arrangement [F]. It shows that these balance laws become the Navier–Stokes equations *when* the arrangement's stress is linear, isotropic and short-memory. That condition is a testable gate located at the jamming margin [O: G-Newton]. It does **not** solve the Clay Millennium problem of global existence and smoothness. It reformulates that problem as a zero-width limit of coarse-grained fields [O]. It does not propose a new turbulence closure or a new numerical method; standard pseudo-spectral solvers are used as measuring instruments. What it adds is an account of *why* fluids flow (the marginal substrate), why cores and patterns have definite sizes (Pillars II–III), and why dissipation survives zero viscosity (Pillar IV, budget level [F]; mechanism verified at finite Reynolds number [V]; infinite-Reynolds limit [O]). The complete list of open gates is in §14.
