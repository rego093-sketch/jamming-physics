# Module 02 — Plastid material is conserved *across* kinds (H1′)

**Author:** Young Jae Lee · ORCID 0009-0002-7535-8245 · CC BY 4.0 · https://jamming-physics.org/
**Tier:** emergence · **Inherits:** Module 01 · **Status:** measured on 6 real plastomes · **Grade:** [V] (with an honest contrast to animals)

**One line.** Six wildly different plants in different habitats share the same core gene material — even more strongly than a two-kind partition would predict.

---

We fetched **six real plastid genomes** — grasses (rice NC_001320.1, maize NC_001666.2, wheat NC_002762.1) and nightshades (tobacco NC_001879.2, tomato NC_007898.3, potato NC_008096.2) — extracted the **62 orthologous protein-coding genes** present in all six, and read each through the engine (`repro/plastid_partition.py`).

**The honest result.** In the **animal** volume, mtDNA γ *partitioned* the two kinds cleanly (median ~17× separation; band centres ~0.07 apart). **In plants the plastid material does the opposite — it is conserved across the two kinds:**

- grass band centre **1.2821** vs nightshade band centre **1.2809** — a difference of only **0.0012**;
- weak per-gene separation (**median 2.69×**); the 44/62 tiny "clean" gaps do not point consistently, which is why the centres coincide;
- **rbcL** (the carbon-fixing enzyme's gene) reads γ ≈ 1.334–1.343 across all six — a range of just **0.0082** for one gene across six habitats.

**반증 = 발견.** The strong two-kind partition seen in animals **does not reproduce** in plant plastid genes; the photosynthesis machinery is *universally conserved*. This is recorded openly — and it supports the user's intuition even more strongly: **very different-looking plants in different habitats share the same core gene material.**

**Grade [V]:** plastid material conserved within and across kinds; the strong two-kind partition is explicitly NOT claimed for plants.
