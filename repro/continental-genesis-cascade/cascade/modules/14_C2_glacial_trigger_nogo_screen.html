Module 14 — C2 NoGo Screen: Glacial Loading as a Physically Real Trigger of Rupture (H-G → H-A)

Tier: 1 (Recent-Sequence applications branch) · Inherits: Constitution & Blueprint (governance/01), Data Grading Charter (governance/00), the parent geodynamics Atlantic-opening engine (void-suction / unjamming; the Ψ_eff > Ψ_y yield gate; the ~3–32 Pa net driving-stress window; the τ ≈ 9 s re-jamming self-limit), Module 13 (C1), Master Scenario (SCENARIO_recent_sequence.md §S3).

What this module does, in one line. It tests the second causal hand-off of the cascade — can a glaciation episode's mass redistribution trigger the Atlantic rupture? — against a present-tense observable: glacially-induced faulting. The answer is framed honestly as a trigger of an already-loaded (near-critical) system, not a from-scratch fracture, which is exactly the scenario's "shock unjams the loaded spring" logic.

0. The firewall as the screen's operating rule (bidirectional — Art. 3)

  • Admissible (used): mapped present-day fault-scarp geometry (length, height) caused by deglaciation; the mechanical relationship "ice-load change perturbs crustal stress and reactivates near-critical faults" (observation-forced); ice-load stress from measured ice thickness and density (σ = ρ g H).
  • Forbidden (not used), both directions: the absolute ages of the faulting episodes (~9–15 ka, or "less than 600 years," etc.) — named, never load-bearing, in either direction; any reconstructed deglaciation-load history used as a load-bearing input.
  • Not load-bearing: no fitted parameter. The inherited geodynamics gate values (Ψ_y window, τ) are carried from the parent volume, treated as inherited thresholds, not re-derived here.

1. The observable anchor: deglaciation observably ruptures the crust

# Observation proc_grade status
C2-a Glacially-induced fault scarps in Fennoscandia: mapped, present-day topographic steps up to ~160 km long and up to ~30 m high, produced when continental ice loads were reduced. D0/D1 (mapped scarp geometry; LiDAR/DEM) ACTIVE
C2-b These are pre-existing faults reactivated by the advance/retreat stress perturbation — i.e. ice-mass change modulates the crustal stress field. D2 (observation-forced mechanism) ACTIVE
C2-c Honest mechanical bound: unloading stress alone is generally insufficient to fracture intact rock; it triggers faults already close to failure. So the effect is a trigger of a near-critical (pre-loaded) system, not a from-scratch rupture. D2/D3 (mechanism + modelling) ACTIVE
C2-d Some scarps record extraordinary single displacements (~30 m), far larger than any historical fault movement in the region; whole-crustal rupture has been related to rebound. D1 (measured displacement) ACTIVE
C2-e Ice-load stress magnitude: for ice 1–3 km thick, the vertical load is σ = ρ_ice g H ≈ 9–27 MPa (order ~10⁷ Pa). Flexural/rebound stresses are smaller (order ~MPa). D2 (forced by measured ρ, g, H) ACTIVE
C2-f Absolute ages of the faulting episodes. D5 (chronology) RECORD (F — firewall, both directions)

Reading. The coupling the scenario needs — a glaciation episode's mass change perturbs the crustal stress field enough to rupture it — is not hypothetical; it is mapped on the ground (C2-a, C2-d). The honest qualifier (C2-c) is not a weakness but a structural match: the rupture is a trigger of an already-loaded system, precisely the scenario's metastable-spring picture (S0 loaded → a shock unjams it). Glacial loading is therefore registered as one physically real instance of the "shock" class that the scenario's S3 invokes (alongside impact or internal stress).

2. Applying it to the C2 hand-off (glacial loading → Atlantic engine)

The inherited geodynamics engine ruptures when the effective stress exceeds the yield stress (Ψ_eff > Ψ_y), with a small net driving-stress window and a fast self-limiting re-jamming. The scenario's C2 claim is that glacial loading/unloading supplies the perturbation that crosses Ψ_y for a rift already near criticality.

  • Screen result — trigger sufficiency: glacial loading produces stress perturbations (order ~MPa to ~tens of MPa, C2-e) that are observably sufficient to rupture near-critical crust (C2-a/d). Crossing a yield threshold by perturbing an already-loaded system therefore requires no physically absurd parameter → not a STOP.
  • The honest conditional (the open part): whether the specific Atlantic rift was near-critical at the relevant moment is [O] — it depends on the inherited engine's pre-loaded state, which is itself a parent-volume premise held open. The screen shows the trigger mechanism is real and sufficient for near-critical systems; it does not show the Atlantic rift was in that state. That is the inherited [O].
  • Self-limit inherited: the τ ≈ 9 s re-jamming self-limit (parent volume) keeps any triggered rupture from running away — consistent with a rapid-onset, self-tapering event (mirrors C1's self-limit in Module 13).

3. Verdict

C2's trigger mechanism is PHYSICALLY PERMITTED (Art. 4.3; Art. 5 UNLOCK): glacial mass redistribution observably ruptures near-critical crust, so "a glaciation episode triggers the Atlantic rupture" needs no STOP-level parameter — conditional on the rift being near-critical, which is the inherited open premise. Occurrence of the specific rupture remains [O].

This completes the two-hand-off NoGo screening of the climate cascade:

  • C1 (warm ocean → rapid glaciation): rate-capability permitted (Module 13).
  • C2 (glaciation → rupture trigger): trigger mechanism permitted (this module).

Both hand-offs are physically permitted, not forbidden; both keep occurrence [O]; both rest on present-tense observables (lake/sea-effect snow + observed snowfall rates for C1; mapped glacially-induced faults for C2), with no chronology load-bearing.

4. Open sub-gates (HOLD — reported, not hidden)

  1. Near-criticality of the Atlantic rift. The trigger works on a pre-loaded system; whether the Atlantic margin was near Ψ_y is the inherited parent-volume premise, [O].
  2. Magnitude matching. A 160-km fault scarp is large but is not an ocean-basin rupture; scaling the observed trigger to a continental-rifting event is an extrapolation (the observed analogue bounds the mechanism, not the scale). HOLD.
  3. Ordering seam (B.5). The pre-opening glaciation (H-G) vs. the geodynamics volume's post-opening hydrological/glacial responses (P16/P19/P20/P24) remain an unreconciled seam (Constitution B.5; author currently holding). This module does not resolve it.
