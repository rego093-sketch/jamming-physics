Module 23 — Fossil-count audit: raw counts, no imported model (an open strand)

Author: Young Jae Lee · ORCID 0009-0002-7535-8245 · CC BY 4.0 · https://jamming-physics.org/
Tier: biology↔history bridge (ongoing) · Inherits: Module 22; GOVERNANCE Art. 7. Data: p3k14c global radiocarbon database (Bird et al. 2022), carried under ../fossil_audit/.
Status: OPEN. Per author note ("the DNA-fossil research needs more work"). The counts are settled; their cause is not.
Standing grade: counts [V]; cause [O], symmetrically.

One line. Asked whether human/animal remains are sparse from ~10,000 BP to ~2,300 BC, the audit answers with raw counts only: yes, ancient remains are genuinely sparse in absolute number ([V]), and the count even drops toward the present (the fingerprint of recent under-dating, [V]) — but no causal story (flood, taphonomy, demography) is decided by counts ([O]), and importing one to settle the meaning would itself violate Art. 7.

1. The counts (recomputed from source, [V])

Over 179,689 real radiocarbon dates and the 1,795 self-tagged as human remains (../fossil_audit/fossil_counts.py):

  • Early-Holocene human-remains dates are single dozens per millennium (e.g. 25 at 9–10k BP, 22 at 10–11k, 13 at 11–12k) versus hundreds at 2,000–5,000 BP (peak 341 at 2–3k). Ancient remains are genuinely sparse in absolute terms — the original observation is correct.
  • The count drops toward the present (54 in 0–500 BP; peak at 2,000–3,000 BP). Under uniform dating + more recent people + better preservation the most recent bin should be richest; it is not. This is the fingerprint of recent remains being under-dated — a known-recent skeleton is not ¹⁴C-dated.

2. What the counts do and do not establish

  • [V] ancient sparseness; [V] recent under-dating signature.
  • [L] correcting for the under-dating would raise recent counts and make the ancient/recent contrast larger — consistent with "ancient genuinely sparser."
  • [O] DISCOVERED ≠ EXISTING: the database counts dated samples, not existing remains; whether sparseness is true scarcity or preservation/discovery loss is undecided.
  • [O] the cause — flood, or low early-Holocene population, or preservation — is not decided by counts. A small ancient population predicts the same sparseness, so counts do not discriminate.
  • [O] strong sampling-rate claims (e.g. "0.01% vs 100% dated") need an existence denominator absent here.

3. The audit posture (Art. 7, demonstrated)

This module is the recorded case behind GOVERNANCE Article 7. Two errors were made and corrected in the open: a data claim asserted from memory ("densely documented" — wrong; withdrawn after counting), and an interpretation imported asymmetrically (taphonomy installed as the answer while flood was held [O] — corrected by demoting every causal model to [O]). The counts are the only [V] object; all causes route to the history channel.

4. Standing — open

This strand is deliberately unfinished. Settled: the counts and their grading. Not settled: an existence-calibrated sampling model and any causal attribution. Both are deferred — to further data and to the history channel — and held open, honestly.

Grade: [V] counts; [L] under-dating direction; [O] existence and cause, symmetrically.
