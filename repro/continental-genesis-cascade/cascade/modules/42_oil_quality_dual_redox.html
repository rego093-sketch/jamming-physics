Module 42 — Oil quality from the glaciation cascade: the dual-redox fingerprint (6/6)

In plain terms. The same meltwater that makes oil's source rock also sets the oil's quality — by doing opposite things at two depths. Deep down it seals off oxygen, preserving the organic source. Up shallow, cold oxygen-bearing meltwater seeps into the reservoir and lets microbes eat the light parts of the oil, leaving it heavy. So Athabasca's tar sands (cold, shallow, meltwater-flushed) are extra-heavy, while the North Sea (deep, hot) is light — and the model recovers all six real provinces from their settings alone.

One line. If the cascade controls the environment, it should control the oil quality too. The driver is the glaciation: meltwater plays a dual redox role — at the deep source a freshwater lid sequesters oxygen (anoxia → good source rock); at the shallow reservoir cool oxygen-bearing recharge does the reverse (biodegradation → heavy oil). From three present-tense knobs (reservoir temperature, meltwater recharge, sulfur potential) the model recovers 6/6 provinces' heavy/light + sour/sweet class: Athabasca extra-heavy, North Sea light, Arabian sour, West Siberia light, Orinoco extra-heavy, Monterey heavy-sour.

1. The dual-redox mechanism

One oxygen knob, two depths, opposite outcomes. Deep source: meltwater lid sequesters O₂ → anoxia → organic preservation → good source rock. Shallow reservoir: cool, oxygen/nutrient-bearing meltwater recharge → microbes biodegrade the oil (eat the light ends) → heavy oil. Biodegradation shuts off above ~80 °C (microbes die), so deep/hot reservoirs stay light.

2. Three present-tense knobs → quality

From repro/data/oil_quality_provinces.csv: reservoir temperature (depth: hot/deep → lighter, and biodegradation off), meltwater recharge × cool reservoir → biodegradation → heavier, and sulfur potential (evaporite-sulfate or Type II-S) → sour. The model predicts each province's class and compares to the observed quality.

3. The result — 6/6

Athabasca (57°N, cool, meltwater-flushed, shallow) → extra-heavy; North Sea (58°N but deep, hot) → light sweet — latitude alone does not set quality; depth/temp + recharge do. Arabian carbonate+anhydrite → sour; Monterey Type II-S → heavy sour. 6/6 provinces recovered from environment alone.
