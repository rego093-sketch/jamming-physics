Module 28 — Source-rock organic richness: the burial budget, the dilution paradox, and the cascade's single-event economy

One line. A petroleum source rock is improbably organic-rich: ordinary oxygenated seafloor buries only a fraction of a percent of organic carbon, yet real source rocks carry several to tens of percent. Reaching that forces three conditions at once — a large organic supply, anoxic preservation, and low mineral dilution — and that requirement is firewall-clean and forced. The author's instinct that this is a deep puzzle is correct and quantified here. But two honest points sharpen it into a strength rather than an over-reach: (1) "impossible without a flood" is too strong — sustained anoxic basins (observed today) also reach high organic content slowly; and (2) a naive "a flood dumps the organics" runs into the dilution paradox — fast mineral burial dilutes the organic fraction. The cascade's genuine advantage is not raw catastrophe but economy: a single coupled event delivers all three conditions together — a mass-mortality organic pulse that outpaces mineral dilution, a warm ocean whose decay oxygen-demand self-generates the anoxia, and rift basins that supply the low dilution — whereas the mainstream needs these independent conditions to coincide, sustained, by luck. That economy is the strongest card; it is parsimony, not proof. Occurrence stays [O], both directions.

1. The puzzle, quantified (repro/source_rock_organic_budget.py)

Total organic carbon (TOC) is an organic-carbon weight fraction, a present-tense measured property of the rock. The screen is a present-tense mass balance over measured fluxes (organic-carbon rain, burial efficiency, bulk mineral sedimentation) — the same class of input as the observed snowfall rate used in Module 25. No absolute age or reconstructed past rate enters.

TOC = (organic-C rain × burial efficiency) / (mineral flux + organic-matter mass).

Four present-day flux regimes:

Regime resulting TOC
oxic, open ocean, normal dilution ~0.05 %
anoxic, normal supply, normal dilution ~1 %
anoxic, normal supply, low dilution ~6 %
anoxic, high supply, low dilution ~31 %

And the requirement, stated as the dilution constraint:

to reach TOC buried organic-C must be ≥ this fraction of mineral flux
1 % ~1 %
5 % ~5.6 %
10 % ~12.5 %
20 % ~33 %

Reading. Ordinary oxygenated burial yields ~0.05 % TOC — average shale. Real source rocks carry several to tens of percent. To get there, the buried organic-carbon flux must be a large fraction of the mineral flux — for a rich rock, comparable to the entire mineral input, versus the ~0.05 % of normal background. That is a hundred-fold-and-more enhancement. The author is right: source-rock richness is a genuine, large puzzle, and it is forced by the mass balance.

2. The two honest points (Art. 7) — they make the argument stronger, not weaker

(a) "Impossible without a flood/glacial event" is too strong. Sustained anoxic basins exist today (e.g. a restricted, stratified, euxinic basin) and slowly accumulate organic-rich mud; widespread anoxic intervals in the record do the same. So high TOC does not logically require a catastrophe — the bare "you need enhancement" conclusion is shared with the mainstream (its anoxia/preservation school). Grading "requires a flood" as forced would be a firewall breach in the cascade's own favour (the mirror image of trusting a mainstream date). It stays [O]. Holding this honestly is what keeps the rest credible.

(b) The dilution paradox cuts against the naive picture. Note where the mineral flux sits in the mass balance: in the denominator. A catastrophic event that dumps a huge load of mineral sediment would dilute the organic fraction toward low TOC, not high. So "a great flood buries lots of mud, therefore rich source rock" is physically backwards as stated. Fast mineral burial helps preservation (less oxygen exposure) but hurts richness (more dilution) — a real tension the mainstream also faces. A rich source rock needs the organic supply to outpace the mineral dilution, or burial in a low-clastic setting.

3. The cascade's genuine advantage — economy and self-consistency, framed correctly

The advantage is not raw catastrophe. It is that a single coupled event supplies, together and from one cause, the three conditions that a rich source rock independently requires — and resolves the dilution paradox by mechanism rather than by luck:

  1. Organic supply that outpaces dilution. A flood-scale mass mortality delivers an organic pulse far above background primary production — large enough that the organic fraction stays high even with mineral input. This is the dilution-paradox resolution the naive picture lacked: the organic flux dominates, rather than being swamped.
  2. Self-generated anoxia. A suddenly warmed ocean holds less oxygen and is strongly stratified; the oxygen demand of decaying the mass-mortality pulse consumes the remaining oxygen. The anoxia is produced by the event itself, not imposed externally and maintained by luck. This is mechanistically different from the mainstream's externally-restricted, sustained anoxic basin.
  3. Low dilution. The rift basins opening in the same relaxation are starved, low-clastic settings — supplying the low mineral dilution the budget demands.

So the cascade buys supply + preservation + low-dilution from one parameter and one event, with the anoxia self-generated and the dilution paradox resolved by an overwhelming organic pulse. The mainstream must have all three coincide, sustained, as independent strokes of luck (the right productivity, the right basin restriction, the right sedimentation, held for a long interval). This is consilience-from-economy — the cascade's signature virtue (Module 16) — and on the source-rock problem it is the strongest distinctive card. It is a reason to prefer, not a proof; it stays [L], and occurrence stays [O].

On the "much fiction" point. The mainstream's per-deposit source-rock stories (which sustained anoxia, which productivity, which restriction, for each rich rock — and, for the world's few giant source intervals, a near-global "oceanic anoxic event" with its own triggered coincidence) are genuinely under-constrained and model-laden. The author's skepticism there has merit. But it is recorded as the mainstream carries a heavy coincidence burden, not as the mainstream is refuted — both occurrences remain [O], and the cascade's claim is the more economical reading of the same forced requirement.

4. Where this is testable next (closing toward a discriminator)

Like the cracking clock (Module 27), the bare budget is degenerate; its discriminating power grows when paired with present-tense observations the sustained-coincidence picture struggles to give together:

  1. Scale and near-synchrony of the world's giant source rocks. A disproportionate share of the world's petroleum is sourced by a few brief intervals. A single coupled event predicts a near-global, near-synchronous, warm-anoxic organic-burial signature; the mainstream predicts independent, basin-by-basin anoxic episodes that happen to cluster. The shared warm-anoxic signature and the geographic breadth are present-tense observables (the relative correlation, not absolute dates). This is the high-leverage test.
  2. The dilution-paradox check itself. In a candidate rich source rock, is the organic accumulation consistent with an organic pulse that outpaced mineral input (supporting the mass-mortality reading), or with slow accumulation under intermediate sedimentation (the sustained reading)? Present-tense sedimentological and organic-petrographic observables bear on this.
  3. Couple to Module 27. A rich source rock that is also over-mature (spent) beneath uncracked light oil at temperature ties the two strands: the source can no longer be charging (Module 27 §3), and its original richness demanded the three-condition event (this module). The convergence is the argument.
