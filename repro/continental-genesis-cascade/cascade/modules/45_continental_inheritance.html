Module 45 — Continental-Genesis inheritance: the conservation identity and the relaxation tail

In plain terms. The cascade's one open question is whether the rifting and salt belong to the same recent event as the ice. A separate study of why dry land exists (Continental Genesis) gives three present-tense, date-free results we can borrow. First, on a fixed-size globe, opening one ocean forces an equal closing somewhere else — and today the plates are observed to balance exactly that way. Second, the mantle is a stiff-but-slow material, so a single present-day speed cannot tell you when it started — “slow now” fits both “old and slow” and “recent and fast, now fading.” That defends the firewall (the date stays open both ways), and gives the magnitudes a reason to track each other — without claiming the rifting is recent.
