1. The trigger seam — resolved by force, not by taste

The conflict (Module 29). One construction (Module 16 §2) has the reservoir release itself collapse the enclosure and open the rupture at the moment of release. The gap/C2 construction (Modules 14, 25) has the rupture triggered by the glacial load built over the ≥100-yr gap. As written, the chain says both.

The resolution is forced. The reading is decided by constraints already sealed elsewhere — not by choice:

  1. The gap floor is strictly positive, and forced. Finite measured snowfall makes instantaneous crustal loading impossible, so the flood→rupture gap is > 0 (Module 25, gate 8235bff6…). A rupture at the moment of release (time zero) therefore contradicts the gap floor.
  2. The gap sits between flood and rupture by construction. The rupture must follow the ice load, which itself accumulates over the gap (Blueprint Part 1). An immediate rupture violates this ordering too.
  3. The release need not rupture to power the opening. In the loaded-spring / R19 picture (Module 16), the release loads the metastable well — it stores configuration energy by removing the enclosure's support; the energy that drives the opening is released when the collapse is triggered, not when the spring is loaded. So "release primes, load triggers, engine drives" loses no energy: the release loads the spring, the glacial load is the perturbation that crosses the ridge, and the relaxation down the potential is the rupture.

The consistency check (repro/seam_resolution_check.py) tests both readings against (1)–(3): the immediate-rupture reading fails (1) and (2); the primes/load-triggers reading passes all three. It is the unique internally-consistent reading. Module 16's "support gone → immediate rupture" wording is therefore demoted to priming language, and the chain is single-triggered.

The honest cost, named and kept [O]. This promotes near-criticality to a load-bearing premise: for the glacial load to trigger the rupture, the rift must be near-critical when loaded (the release having brought it there). This stays [O] — but it is a coherent [O], not a guess:

  • "Primed-but-stuck" is a regime, not a tuned point. The inherited engine is velocity-weakening and stick-or-fast: a near-critical zone is stuck until a perturbation unsticks it. The release need only bring the rift into that range of states; any sufficient perturbation (the glacial load) then triggers. No narrow window is required, so no-tuning is respected.
  • The "release must be sub-critical (does not rupture alone)" requirement is a one-sided bound, not a fine-tuned coincidence — it either holds or it does not, and whether it holds for the specific rift is exactly the [O].

So the resolution removes the contradiction, keeps the single-switch economy, makes the gap necessary, and is consistent with the observed analogue (deglaciation faulting triggers near-critical faults — load-triggering, not release-rupture). What remains open (was this rift so primed?) is stated as a coherent open premise, not asserted.

2. The ordering seam — one timeline under an explicit condition

The conflict (Module 29). The cascade places glaciation before the opening (it loads the crust); the inherited geodynamics volume describes responses after the opening. As written, it was unclear whether these compete.

The resolution. They occupy different positions in one relaxation, so they do not compete by construction:

release (loads the spring)
  → flood + warm ocean
  → [gap ≥ 100 yr : ice builds, loads the crust]      (PRE-rupture — the cascade's glaciation)
  → glacial load TRIGGERS the rupture
  → opening
  → inherited post-opening responses                  (POST-rupture — the inherited volume)

The cascade's glaciation and the inherited responses are on opposite sides of the rupture; one timeline holds both. The seam closes provided one explicit condition, stated rather than assumed:

Consistency condition. No inherited post-opening response may presuppose an ice-free pre-rupture state. None is apparent (the opening dynamics, the thermal relaxation, and petroleum formation do not require the prior absence of ice). This should be verified against the inherited volume's content; if any response is found to require an ice-free pre-state, the conflict is real and the honest fix is to revise the pre-loading magnitude — not to ignore the conflict (Art. 7).

Stating the condition, rather than declaring consistency, is the grounded move: the seam is closed conditionally and checkably, with the failure branch (revise, don't ignore) pre-registered.
