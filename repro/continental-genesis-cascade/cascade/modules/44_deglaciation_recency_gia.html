Module 44 — Deglaciation recency from ongoing rebound (present-tense): corrects M35

In plain terms. Hudson Bay and Scandinavia are still rising today — the ground is springing back after heavy ice was removed. A springy-but-slow mantle relaxes over thousands of years; if the ice had gone hundreds of millions of years ago, the land would already sit flat with no rise left. The fact that it is rising now means the ice melted recently, on the order of ten thousand years. This needs no calendar date and no assumed decay rate — just the springback we measure today. It is a present-tense fact about the ice, not a claim that the flood is proven recent.

1. The physics (present-tense)

A viscous mantle under a removed load relaxes with a characteristic time τ = 4πη/(ρgλ) (fundamental mode, load wavelength λ). After time t since unloading the remaining uplift decays as exp(−t/τ). If t ~ a few τ, uplift is still going; if t » τ (e.g. 10⁸ yr = thousands of τ), it is complete. We measure ~1 cm/yr of uplift today in the old ice centres → unloading was within ~1–2 τ.

2. The verdict

Rebound is still going, so with τ of order 10⁴ yr the deglaciation was recent, order 10⁴ yr. This is exactly the author's “last ice age” logic, and it needs no calendar date and no assumed decay rate — just the ongoing springback we measure now. [V].
