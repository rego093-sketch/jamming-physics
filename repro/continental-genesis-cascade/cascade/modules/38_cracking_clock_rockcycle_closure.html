Module 38 — Cracking-clock closure: the geological rock-cycle clock binds, Gyr-retention refuted

In plain terms. People say oil can survive a billion years in a hot reservoir, so its presence proves nothing about timing. But a billion-year survival has to survive the rock cycle too — and traps and source rocks do not last that long (they get buried and turned to gas/graphite, or lifted up and eroded). So the “billion-year retention” idea is refuted by the slower of the two clocks. That said, the rock-cycle clock still allows very long times, so the absolute age stays open either way.

1. Two clocks, not one

Thermal clock: at ~90 °C oil→gas cracking is slow (~Gyr). Taken alone it buys no recency edge. Geological clock: the trap and the rock do not survive a Gyr — the rock cycle either buries them deeper (oil→gas→graphite) or exhumes and erodes them, on ~10⁷–10⁸ yr. The binding clock is the geological one.

2. The correction (mark-never-erase)

The prior turn said “oil survives deep time at ~90 °C, no problem” — a one-sided thermal-only reading. It ignored the rock cycle. The author's Persian-Gulf / Babylon objection (a thing that survives thermally must still survive the rock cycle, which it does not) is conceded. Gyr-retention is refuted; the original wording stands with this two-clock correction appended.
