Module 47 — Causal closure: the value criterion (one untuned cause vs many)

In plain terms. You can’t prove what happened in the deep past with any one measurement we can make today — and three careful tests showed exactly that. That isn’t a failure; it’s the honest limit. What really separates this framework from the textbook is something bigger: the textbook explains the flood, the ice age, the ocean opening, and each oil field with separate causes (eight of them), each tuned to fit. This framework explains all of them with one cause — a single deep release — and zero numbers fitted to the data. When two stories both fit what we see, the one that needs one untuned cause instead of eight is the better bet. That economy — closing the whole thing causally, without tuning — is the real value.

The recent-sequence cascade was repeatedly measured by the wrong test — can one present-tense observable cleanly separate it from the mainstream? What actually distinguishes the frameworks is a global property, not a per-axis one: who closes the whole system causally, integratively, without tuning.
