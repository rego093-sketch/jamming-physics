Glossary — the terms in plain words

Plain definitions of the recurring vocabulary. The rigorous statements live in Governance; this page is the easy reference.

The four grades

GradePlain meaning
[F] forced / derivedIt follows necessarily from measured constants and the engine — you cannot avoid it.
[V] verifiedMeasured directly today, or reproduced by a fixed-seed program.
[L] leaningA well-anchored inference — reasonable, but not yet forced.
[O] openUndecided. Includes every claim that something happened in the past — those are open forever, by rule.

Order of strength: F > V > L > O. Nothing downstream may quietly turn an open item into a stronger one.

The firewall (both directions)

The core rule: calendar dates, ages, durations, and rates are never used as proof — not to argue “old” and not to argue “recent.” They are kept as record only. The firewall is symmetric: it removes the mainstream's dates and withholds the convenient large model numbers, so nothing is inflated in either direction. Only things measurable today (present-tense observables) can carry an argument.

Present-tense vs occurrence

Present-tense = something you can measure on the Earth now (a rock's layering, a basin's shape, the land still rising). Occurrence = a claim that an event happened in the past. Present-tense facts can be strong ([V]); occurrence claims are capped at [O]. Subtle but important: “the ice melt was recent” is read from an ongoing measurement (the land is rising now), so it is present-tense — it does not mean the flood is proven recent.

Key terms

TermPlain meaning
R19 bistable switchThe one engine behind everything: a jammed (stuck, load-bearing) state that can flip to unjammed (released, flowing). “Jammed ⇔ unjammed.”
c² = B/ρThe VP foundation's core relation (the speed of light as an elastic-wave speed of the vacuum lattice). It is why the deep material can be solid yet flow.
Dual-redoxGlacial meltwater doing opposite things to oxygen at two depths: sealing it out deep (preserving source rock), bringing it in shallow (making oil heavy).
Source rockThe organic-rich rock that later generates oil. Needs a quiet, oxygen-starved (anoxic) setting to preserve the organic matter.
Anoxia / freshwater lidA layer of fresh (melt) water sitting on heavier seawater stops oxygen from reaching the bottom → the bottom goes oxygen-free (anoxic) → organic matter is preserved.
BiodegradationMicrobes eating the light parts of oil, leaving it heavy. Happens in cool, shallow reservoirs reached by oxygen-bearing water; shuts off above ~80 °C.
GIA / forebulge / marine limitGlacial Isostatic Adjustment: the crust bending under ice and springing back after. The forebulge is the bulge just outside the ice; the marine limit is the highest old shoreline, marking the former load.
Maxwell timeThe time that decides whether a material acts solid or fluid. The mantle is solid over seconds (it carries earthquake S-waves) but flows over millions of years (it convects).
Stick-slip / duty cycleMotion that is mostly stuck with rare fast slips. A slow average speed can be rare fast events spread thinly in time — so a present speed cannot, by itself, tell you when it started.
Conservation identityOn a fixed-size globe, opening one ocean must be balanced by closing somewhere else — an exact bookkeeping fact, true at any speed.
Double-SHA-256 gateA self-check inside each program: it hashes its own output twice and refuses to pass unless the hash matches the pinned value — proof the result has not drifted.
SEED = 19The fixed random seed convention, so every computation is deterministic and reproducible.
Mark-never-eraseCorrections are appended and marked, never silently deleted — so the record shows what changed and why.
