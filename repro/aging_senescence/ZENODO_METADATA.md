## Title

Aging and Senescence: the Systemic Decline of Homeostatic Setpoints over the Lifespan

## Description

Aging is read on the VP substrate as the systemic, monotonic decline of γ-defended homeostatic setpoints.
From sequence alone the promoter γ of the aging masters (TP53, CDKN2A, FOXO3, TERT) is measured (−mean
nearest-neighbour stacking ΔG) and fixes each node's barrier γ²/4 and reservoir dwell ∼ γ^1.5; the dynamics
— setpoint drift into a discontinuous failure, the stuck cellular-senescence attractor, finite-reservoir
depletion, a convex age risk-multiplier, and a shared rate of aging — are deterministic simulations seeded
at 19. A cross-species discriminant finds that human aging genes are not special and that the longevity
switch is TP53 copy number, not promoter γ. An observation-only archaic↔present-day comparison, reconstructed
from high-coverage Neanderthal, Denisovan, and ancient-modern genomes, finds the four masters read the same
γ in dated individuals (with the senescence gate identical in dated modern humans and TERT carrying the most
archaic substitutions). A telomere deep-dive locates the telomere as the keystone of aging dynamics, not γ:
the canonical telomere repeat γ is a near-exact universal constant and its lever acts off the promoter-γ
axis, through reservoir length and attrition rate.

This version adds the paper's closing conclusion: the genome fixes the ruler but not the realized lifespan.
The realized-lifespan gap is shown to be underdetermination rather than unfinished computation — across a
fifty-fold cross-species lifespan span the flattest master promoter γ barely moves (TP53 CV 3.5%), the
telomere lever sits off the γ axis, and the cross-species null is audited robust to GC, body-mass,
phylogenetic, and multivariate confounds — so the lifespan lever lives off-axis in dosage and reservoir
dynamics, and an external clock (not more computation on the promoter) is what the absolute calendar rates
would require. The work follows a LOCK → Derive → Gate discipline with no tuning, bit-for-bit reproduction
(SEED=19, two runs byte-identical), and honest grading ([V] verified / [L] cited / [O] open-with-obstacle).
