## How a disease maps onto the substrate
Every entry is framed VP-natively as **a perturbation of an existing module** or **a request for one new
primitive**. The substrate primitives already present are: the R19 bistable switch `ds/dt = g·s − s³ + h`
(fate/secretion/barrier), the FitzHugh–Nagumo slow-wave oscillator (rhythm), weak Kuramoto coupling
(propagation), a mass-conserving flux (transport), and a graded switch-population control loop
(homeostat). A disease either (a) moves a parameter of one of these (Tier 1), (b) needs one small new
element — a sphincter control-loop, a migrating-motor-complex sweep, an accommodation reservoir, an
afferent-gain term (Tier 2), or (c) needs a whole new layer the substrate does not yet contain — immune
/ inflammation, exocrine secretion + autodigestion, perfusion/vascular, lipid handling, wall mechanics
(Tier 3, honest `[O]` with a stated obstacle).

## Grading convention (same as the built modules)
`[F]` forced · `[V]` simulation-verified · `[L]` cited anchor · `[O]` open (obstacle stated). A module
ships **only** when its discriminant passes under wide sweeps; writing stays locked until then
(research-first). New primitives must be added to the substrate single-source or vendored — never fitted
ad hoc. Every `[O]` goes in `IRREPRODUCIBILITY_LEDGER.md` with its obstacle.

## Seam / firewall discipline (unchanged)
Brain-gut and felt/visceral-sensation components stay in `mind` (HPA is never re-emerged here). Hepatic
**structural** disease and GI bleeding overlap the `circulatory` sibling and are **cited, not
re-emerged** (liver identity is SSOT-owned by DNA/circulatory; only the glucose/glycogen metabolic role
is owned here). Organ identity/order stay SSOT in `dna`.
