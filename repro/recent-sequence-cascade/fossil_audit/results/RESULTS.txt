------------------------------------------------------------------------
GRADED FINDINGS (audit principle: counts [V]; every cause [O], symmetrically)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [V]  early-Holocene human-remains counts are genuinely small in absolute terms
       (single dozens per millennium at 8-12k BP vs hundreds at 2-5k BP).
  [V]  the count DROPS toward the present (fewest in 0-500 BP), the fingerprint of
       recent remains being under-dated -- you do not 14C-date a known-recent skeleton.
  [L]  correcting for that under-sampling would raise recent counts and make the
       ancient/recent contrast LARGER -- consistent with 'ancient genuinely sparser'.
  [O]  whether sparseness reflects true scarcity vs preservation/discovery loss:
       DISCOVERED != EXISTING; this database counts DATED samples, not existing remains.
  [O]  the CAUSE of ancient sparseness -- flood, OR low ancient population, OR
       preservation -- is NOT decided by counts. Low early-Holocene population predicts
       the same sparseness, so the counts do not discriminate. Cause -> history channel.
  [O]  strong sampling-rate claims (e.g. '0.01% vs 100% dated') need an existence
       denominator absent from this database.
  NOTE: no external model (taphonomy, flood, demography) is imported to explain the
        counts. Importing one to settle the meaning would itself violate Art. 7.
