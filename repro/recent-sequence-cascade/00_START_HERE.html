Start here — the cascade in plain language

This page has no jargon. It is the easy door into the package. Each idea is one short paragraph. When you want the rigorous version with grades, gates, and data, follow the links.

The VP Recent-Sequence Cascade asks one question: could a flood, an ice age, and an ocean opening all be one event — a single release of a deep, high-pressure water reservoir — rather than three unrelated things spread across deep time? It does not claim this happened; it asks whether the physics permits it, and which parts can be checked today.

The one idea

Deep in the Earth, water can be stored as a high-pressure solid (this is real — it has been found inside diamonds). Think of it as a loaded spring. If it releases, the same release can show three faces: water reaching the surface (a flood), the released heat warming the ocean so it snows heavily and builds ice (an ice age), and the collapse of the reservoir's container helping an ocean basin open. One cause, three faces.

The rules we hold ourselves to

We never use calendar dates as proof — not “old” and not “recent.” Only things we can measure today carry the argument. When a question really turns on timing, the honest answer is “open,” both ways. Every number is reproducible by a small program that checks itself. And when we get something wrong, we mark the correction; we don't erase it. (The short name for the whole rule set is the firewall; plain definitions are in the glossary.)

Where the oil comes in

The best-developed part of the cascade is about petroleum. The key insight: the meltwater from the ice age does two opposite things at two depths — deep down it preserves the organic matter that becomes oil's source rock; up shallow it makes the oil heavy. That one idea predicts where the world's oil is and what kind it is, and eight separate present-day checks agree. See the petroleum overview.

The one thing we can say about timing

Hudson Bay and Scandinavia are still rising today because heavy ice was removed. A slow, springy mantle would already be flat if that happened hundreds of millions of years ago — so the ice melted recently, on the order of ten thousand years. That is a present-day measurement, not a claim that the flood is proven recent (that stays open). See Module 44.

The one open question

If the ice melt was recent, were the oil source rocks, the salt, and the ocean rifting part of that same recent event — or a separate, much older story? That is the single genuine open question. A companion study of why continents exist (Continental Genesis) lends some of its logic here; see Module 45.
