Module 15 — Correction & Re-specification of the Basal-Character Test (the high-latitude "slow marine" reading was an over-generalization; the world's largest source rock carries cold-water indicators)

Author: Young Jae Lee · ORCID 0009-0002-7535-8245 · CC BY 4.0 · https://jamming-physics.org/
Tier: 1 (Recent-Sequence applications branch) · Inherits: Constitution & Blueprint (governance/01), Data Grading Charter (governance/00), Correction Ledger (governance/11 Part A, esp. E8), Oil model (oil_model/README), Blueprint v2 (CASCADE_BLUEPRINT_and_GRADED_LEDGER_v2).
Status: CORRECTION + DATA module. Retracts an over-generalization the assistant made about the decisive petroleum test, brings new firewall-admissible data, and re-specifies the test as multi-dimensional.
Standing grade: occurrence/age remain [O] (Art. 4). This module changes a grading, not an occurrence claim.

What this module does, in one line. The assistant had framed the high-latitude source-rock texture ("laminated marine shale, not chaotic till") as unfavourable to the glacial/flood-rapid reading. The author correctly objected that cold high-latitude glacial systems move slowly and deposit fine laminated mud that looks like slow marine sedimentation — so lamination cannot discriminate. New data confirms more: the world's largest source rock (Bazhenov) carries cold-water indicators. The "unfavourable" verdict is therefore retracted and the test re-specified along three independent axes.

PART A — Assistant correction (inherits governance/11 Part A format)

[E10] — Framed "laminated source-rock = slow marine = unfavourable to a glacial/flood origin"

  • Claimed (prior session report, and in Blueprint v2): that the high-latitude source rocks (Bazhenov, Kimmeridge) being laminated marine shale rather than chaotic till leans unfavourable to the glacial-transport reading, making the basal-character test "decisive and currently unfavourable."
  • Confident because: (a) the unexamined mainstream default that "source rocks are quiet low-energy marine," and (b) a conflation of "glacial" with high-energy chaotic till at the ice front, so that "laminated/quiet" read as "not glacial."
  • Retracted because — and this re-commits the project's own E8:
    1. E8 (already in the ledger): glacial meltwater carries fine suspended mud that settles in low-energy distal basins (proglacial lakes, glaciomarine shelves), producing finely laminated sediment — varves. Cold high-latitude / slow-moving (cold-based) ice and deglacial meltwater pulses deposit quiet, fine, laminated sediment. So "laminated/quiet ⇒ not glacial" is exactly the conflation E8 already retracted; the assistant partly re-made it.
    2. Lamination is a standard glacial facies, hence degenerate. Glaciolacustrine and glaciomarine sediments are typically laminated on the millimetre scale; "laminated, normal-graded, conformable, with dropstones" are interpretation criteria for both glaciomarine and glaciolacustrine settings. Texture alone does not separate glacial from slow-marine.
    3. Empirically wrong for the actual rock (see Part B): the Bazhenov Formation carries glendonites (cold-water mineral pseudomorphs) and a cold-Arctic-shelf geochemical analogue — i.e. the real rock points to a cold high-latitude setting, not the warm low-latitude marine setting the "unfavourable" framing implicitly assumed.
  • Status: RETRACTED. The Blueprint-v2 line "the petroleum basal-character test currently leans unfavourable" is corrected to "texture is degenerate; the temperature axis leans cold; rate/transport remain open" (Part C). The error is the same asymmetric mainstream-default failure mode the ledger's root-cause names (governance/11).

Reading. The casual "warm anoxic marine" picture of the world's largest source rock is not what the rock's own minerals say: glendonites (OC-a/b) and a Kara-Sea-type analogue (OC-c) indicate a cold, high-latitude shelf. That removes the basis for calling the texture "unfavourable." But the counter-evidence (OC-d) keeps it honest: this reads as a cool, fluctuating high-latitude sea, not a demonstrated ice age.

PART C — The basal-character test, re-specified as THREE independent axes (the multi-dimensional view)

The author's point is that a single texture label collapses several distinct physical questions. Separating them:

Axis Question Current reading Discriminating power
1. Texture (lamination) laminated vs chaotic DEGENERATE — cold/quiet glacial meltwater and slow marine both produce fine laminae none (cannot adjudicate)
2. Temperature warm vs cold water leans COLD — glendonites + Kara-Sea analogue (OC-a/c); caveated by warm fossils (OC-d) supports the high-latitude-cold framing — but cold ≠ glacial-transport ≠ rapid
3. Rate & transport slow pelagic vs rapid glacial/flood delivery; ice-transported vs not OPEN — temperature indicators do not constrain rate; texture is degenerate the decisive axis, and it is unrun

The sharper test that axis 3 demands (the real next experiment). Lamination is degenerate, but dropstones / ice-rafted debris are a positive glacial-transport signal that slow pelagic marine sedimentation cannot produce (outsized clasts dropped from melting icebergs, deforming the laminae beneath them). So the re-specified discriminator is not "laminated vs chaotic" but:

Do the high-latitude source-rock laminae contain dropstones / ice-rafted debris (and what is the grain sorting / provenance)? Presence ⇒ ice transport (supports glacial delivery). Absence ⇒ consistent with either cold-marine-slow or non-iceberg meltwater. Plus an independent rate constraint (event-bedding signatures vs continuous accumulation), since temperature says nothing about rate.

This is the genuinely decisive, firewall-clean, observable test — a pre-registered search for IRD/dropstones + sorting + a rate signature in a representative high-latitude source-rock set, not a map and not a texture binary.

PART D — Honest standing after the correction

  1. The "decisive unfavourable" signal is withdrawn. It rested on an over-generalization (texture degenerate) and was empirically wrong for the actual rock (which is cold, not warm). This is a correction in the hypothesis's favour on the temperature axis.
  2. But it is a re-opening, not a confirmation. Cold-water indicators establish cold, not glacial transport and not rapid deposition. The scenario's distinctive claims (rapid; glacier/flood-delivered organics) remain [O]. A cold epicontinental sea with slow organic-rich mud accumulation remains a fully live alternative and is also [O].
  3. The firewall point is symmetric. The mainstream default "warm marine ⇒ old/slow" was the contaminant here (now stripped); but the inverse "cold ⇒ glacial ⇒ rapid ⇒ recent" would be the mirror contaminant and is not asserted. Temperature ≠ chronology; the absolute age stays [O] both ways.
  4. Net: the high-latitude source-rock evidence moves from "degenerate map + unfavourable texture" to "degenerate texture + cold-leaning temperature + open rate/transport, decided by an unrun dropstone/IRD + rate test." More interesting, more open, and pointed at a sharper experiment.

  • Glendonites in Bazhenov: first-findings literature (Frolovskaya Megadepression, Ryazanian interval); regional Arctic glendonite/cooling studies (Western Taimyr / Yenisei-Khatanga); glendonite-temperature caveat (synthetic ikaite ~35 °C) noted from the Nordic-Seas Eocene literature.
