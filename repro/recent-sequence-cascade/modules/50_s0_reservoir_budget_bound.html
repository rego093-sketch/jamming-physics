Module 50 — SH-5 reservoir-budget bound: the dominant [O] root, bounded present-tense

In plain terms. The whole chain has always leaned on one demanding question: is there enough deep water for the flood and the ice? This module answers the part you can answer today. Diamonds bring up a mineral (ringwoodite) that is about 1.4% water by weight, and estimates put the water stored deep inside the Earth at roughly one to two oceans' worth. That is about ten times more than even the largest amount the chain needs. So "is there enough water down there?" is no longer the sticking point — there is. What stays genuinely open is a different question: how much of that buried water could actually be released to the surface in one event, and by what mechanism. That part has no present-day measurement, so it stays open. The bottleneck didn't vanish — it got smaller and more precise.

One line. SH-5 — reservoir sufficiency — is the bottleneck the whole chain converges on. Bounded by present-tense magnitudes: the deep-water inventory (ringwoodite ~1.4 wt%; transition-zone/bulk water of order ~1–2 ocean masses, 1 ocean mass ≈ 1.4×10²¹ kg) exceeds even the largest water requirement by ~10×. So the inventory is not the binding constraint [V]; the residual open question narrows to the releasable fraction + mechanism, which stay [O]. SH-5 is reframed, not closed: inventory ample, release unknown.

1. Why this module exists

The whitepaper flags flood-energy sourcing as the demanding link and carries an honest HOLD on it; the roadmap (T2.2) asks for a present-tense measurement [that] bounds the releasable deep-water fraction. SH-5 has been the dominant [O] root that every other link conditions on. This module supplies the present-tense bound the roadmap names — and is careful to bound only what is present-tense, leaving the release itself open.

2. The present-tense inventory (load-bearing [V])

Two kinds of present-tense evidence bound the deep-water inventory: a direct sample — ringwoodite recovered as natural inclusions carries ~1.4 wt% H₂O — and estimates of transition-zone and bulk-mantle water of order ~1–2 ocean masses. None of these is an age or a rate; they are quantities measurable today.

3. The bound (a ratio of present magnitudes)

Against the water requirements — one large ice sheet (~5×10¹⁸ kg), the whole present cryosphere (~2.6×10¹⁹ kg), and an illustrative ~0.1 ocean-mass surface flood pulse (~1.4×10²⁰ kg) — the smallest inventory exceeds the largest requirement by ~10× (~1 order of magnitude). Had the inventory fallen below the requirement, SH-5 would be forbidden [V]; it does not.

4. Interpretation — reframed, not closed

The deep-water inventory is bounded [V] and, in magnitude, is not the binding constraint. The residual open question is therefore narrowed and made precise: what fraction of this inventory can actually be mobilised to the surface in one event, and by what mechanism/rate. No present-tense measurement here bounds a release fraction, and asserting one would import an occurrence — so it stays [O]. SH-5 moves from unbounded bottleneck to inventory ample, release fraction unknown.
