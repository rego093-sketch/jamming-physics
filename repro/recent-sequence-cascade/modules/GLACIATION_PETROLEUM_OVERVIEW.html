Glaciation-Driven Petroleum — overview and scorecard

In plain terms. One idea ties this whole branch together: the meltwater from a glaciation does two opposite things at two depths. Deep down it seals off oxygen and preserves the organic matter that becomes oil's source rock; up shallow it carries oxygen in and lets microbes turn the oil heavy. That single idea predicts both where the world's oil is and what kind it is — and eight independent present-day checks all line up with it.

1. The synthetic core — the dual-redox role of glacial meltwater

The same water does opposite things at two depths. At the deep source it forms a freshwater lid that sequesters oxygen → anoxia → organic preservation → source rock. At the shallow reservoir, cool oxygen-bearing meltwater recharge does the reverse → biodegradation → heavy oil. One agent, one redox knob, two outcomes — and that single idea organizes both where oil is (M41) and what kind it is (M42).

4. The single open frontier (the only genuine remaining task)

One real, physical, unresolved question remains: are the source rocks, salt, and rifting causally part of the same recent relaxation as the (provably recent) deglaciation — or coincidentally a separate deep-time history?

A single metastable relaxation has one timescale: fast onset, slow-decaying tail — exactly what the ongoing rebound proves for the ice (M44). By the same logic, present-day slow rates (Atlantic spreading ~2 cm/yr) could be the decaying tail of a recent fast event rather than evidence of a slow, old process — “slow now” ≠ “slow always + old.” Module 45 imports the Continental-Genesis conservation identity, jammed-Maxwell substrate, and forced-convection loop to turn this into a causal frame and a rigorous two-edged-firewall result — without proving occurrence or recency.
