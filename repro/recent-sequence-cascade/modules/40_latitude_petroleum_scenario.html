Module 40 — Per-latitude deglacial → petroleum-source scenario

In plain terms. Where would oil's source material form best as the ice melts? Three things we can measure today set it: how directly the Sun strikes (hottest at the equator), how much the humid air traps heat (most over warm seas), and where it rains (wet equator, dry deserts near 30°, wet storm tracks near 45°). Put together with the meltwater, the best zones are a strong equatorial peak and a second peak at 45° that beats the dry 30° belt — a double structure a simple temperature average would hide.

1. The three present-tense drivers

Angle of incidence (annual-mean insolation from the present orbit), water-vapour greenhouse (Clausius–Clapeyron on the observed SST field — the warm equator carries ~6.6× the pole's column vapour), and rainfall (the present zonal-mean field, frozen in repro/data/zonal_precip.csv: wet equator ~2000, arid 30° ~500, wet 45° ~1000, dry pole ~150 mm/yr). All present-tense [V].

2. Coupling to the melt

Meltwater + rainfall form a freshwater lid → stratification → bottom-water anoxia → organic preservation; nutrients drive productivity; warm water holds less O₂, reinforcing anoxia. Source burial is strongest where productivity × preservation is greatest, and the window broadens as the lid spreads equatorward during deglaciation.

3. The non-monotonic structure (that an average hides)

A strong equatorial/low-latitude peak (every driver aligns), a secondary 45° high (storm-track rainfall + meltwater lid) that beats the arid 30° desert belt, and minima at the arid subtropics and the cold dry poles. An annual-mean-temperature-only treatment would rank 30° above 45° and erase the rainfall signal.

4. Scope (honest)

The drivers are present-tense [V]; the favourability field is a constructed forward index with transparent weights (in the script header). It makes no claim about occurrence, rate, or absolute timing — those stay [O]. The structure (equatorial peak, mid-latitude secondary, subtropical/polar minima) follows from the driver fields and is robust to reasonable reweighting.
