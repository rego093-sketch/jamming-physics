Module 31 — Petroleum location screen on real curated data (where the world's oil actually sits)

One line. The curated data already exists and is citable. Mann, Gahagan & Gordon (2003, AAPG Memoir 78) classified all 877 of the world's giant oil and gas fields (≈ two-thirds of world reserves) by tectonic setting: passive margin 35 %, rift + sag 31 %, collision/foreland 20 %, other ≤ 14 %. Passive margins are themselves rifted margins, so ~66 % of giant petroleum sits in rifting-associated settings — about twice what a random assignment across the setting classes would give. Sorkhabi (2009) adds that six source-rock intervals (~34 % of the record) hold > 90 % of conventional oil. So petroleum is strongly non-random: concentrated in rifting + salt-bearing margins (Campos, Santos, North Sea, Gulf of Mexico, South Atlantic) and in a few intervals — exactly the suite the cascade predicts (P1, P4). The honest qualifier: the mainstream also expects petroleum at rifted margins (rifting makes the basins, restriction, salt and traps), so co-location alone does not discriminate — the cascade's edge is economy (one event for the whole suite), held at [L], not a knockout. And the six intervals are relatively ordered by superposition — a present-tense fact — so a single event does not straightforwardly explain them; that reading is challenged and must narrow or stay [O].

1. The data (already processed, already cited)

Two published censuses do the processing; this module only reads them.

Tectonic setting of giant fields — Mann, Gahagan & Gordon 2003 (AAPG Memoir 78). The world's 877 giant oil and gas fields (≥ 500 million bbl recoverable), ≈ 67 % of world reserves, cluster in 27 regions (~30 % of land). By dominant setting: passive margin 35 %; continental rift + sag 31 %; terminal collision + foreland 20 %; strike-slip/subduction/other ≤ 14 %.

Source-rock concentration — Sorkhabi 2009 (GeoExpro); Klemme & Ulmishek 1991. Six stratigraphic intervals hold > 90 % of conventional oil and gas while spanning only ~34 % of Phanerozoic time.

(For per-basin depth work the USGS World Petroleum Assessment / NAGA — ~170+ basins, downloadable — is the route; not needed for the headline result here.)

2. The screen (repro/petroleum_location_screen.py)

  • Rifting-associated fraction. Passive margins form by rifting (rift → drift → passive margin), so they and continental rifts are one family: 35 % + 31 % = 66 % of giant petroleum sits in rifting-associated settings.
  • Against a random null. If petroleum were placed at random across the ~6 setting classes, any two classes would carry ~33 %. Observed rifting-associated = 66 % → enrichment ≈ ×2.0. The concentration toward rifting is real, not a counting artifact — [V].
  • Few-interval clustering. Six intervals holding > 90 % of oil is an extreme departure from uniform — petroleum clusters in a few source intervals — [V].

3. Reading — symmetric and honest

What the cascade gets right (present-tense, firewall-clean). The cascade predicts petroleum co-locates with the opening (rifting), with salt, and with organic-rich source rock, all as one engine's residue (P1, P4). The data show exactly that suite: a clear majority of the world's giant petroleum is in rifted margins, and the flagship provinces (Campos, Santos, North Sea, Gulf of Mexico, the South Atlantic conjugate margins) carry rift + salt + rich source together. This is a genuine, curated, present-tense match — [V] for the co-location.

Why it does not, by itself, discriminate. The mainstream expects petroleum at rifted margins for standard reasons: rifting makes subsiding basins, restricted circulation (anoxia + salt), and structural traps. So the co-location is degenerate — both accounts predict it (consistent with the Module 20 structural finding). The cascade's distinctive claim is economy: one event supplies the rift, the salt, the warm anoxic ocean, and the organic pulse together, where the mainstream assembles them over time. That is a reason to prefer (parsimony), not a proof — [L].

The honest challenge to the single-event reading (data vs hypothesis). The source rocks do not cluster in one interval; they cluster in six, and those six are relatively ordered by superposition — a present-tense observable, not merely a set of absolute dates the firewall removes. Six ordered levels are not one event. Per the project's own rule (data conflicts → revise the hypothesis, never the data), the source strand's "single coupled event" reading must narrow — to one engine / one signature (warm-anoxic + rift + salt + organic pulse) that recurs — or be held [O]. It must not be forced. (The cascade's mechanism is untouched by this; what is constrained is only the claim that the deposition was a single episode.) The absolute ages of the six intervals stay RECORD, both directions.

4. What this establishes

The author's bar — explain where petroleum is found, better than the competitor — is partly met and honestly bounded. The cascade matches the present-tense location data (rifting + salt + few-interval concentration) and offers a more economical account of why the whole suite co-occurs; that is its strongest distinctive card, at [L]. It does not win on co-location alone (degenerate), and it is challenged on the single-event reading by the six relatively-ordered source intervals — which is recorded, not hidden, and which narrows the claim rather than refuting the engine. Nothing is forced above its grade.
