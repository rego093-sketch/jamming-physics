Input-contamination robustness — are the theory-laden geophysical inputs load-bearing?

In plain terms. Some of the numbers fed in — how dense the deep rock is, how thick and stiff the plate is — aren’t things anyone has directly weighed; they come out of models, and could even be nudged to fit the standard story. You can’t check them all to the bottom. So instead of asking “are the numbers exactly right?”, ask “would the answer change if they were wrong?” We took the one test that leans on those numbers and re-ran it for every combination across the full plausible range — 315 of them. The answer never changed. So those uncertain numbers don’t carry the result, and there’s no need to verify them one by one. If the answer had flipped, the test would have said so out loud.

One line. The right question about an un-verifiable theory-laden input is not whether it is clean but whether the conclusion depends on it; a full 315-point contamination sweep (Te, E, ρm, ν across their literature ranges, worst-case corners included) leaves both observed forebulge offsets bracketed at every point ⇒ ROBUST, so those inputs are not load-bearing and need no bottom-audit; the load-bearing VP-vs-mainstream claims are dimensionless and never use them. Where an input were load-bearing the same sweep would flag it — verification is delegated and dependency is disclosed, not assumed away.

1. The worry — and why "audit everything" is the wrong goal

Some frozen inputs are not observations. Standard crustal density, continental thickness, effective elastic thickness, mantle density and lithospheric Young’s modulus are theory-laden constructs: an ensemble-average baked through seismic velocity–density relations (themselves empirical fits) and isostasy/gravity inversions (non-unique, so a regularisation choice enters). Some may even be quietly corrected toward a standard picture — importing mainstream assumptions through the back door. Auditing every such input to the bottom is impossible (even one seismometer trace is unreadable without wave theory and instrument correction; theory-ladenness has no exit).

So the right question is not “is the input clean?” (unanswerable) but “does the conclusion depend on the input being clean?” Of dozens of inputs, only a few actually move a conclusion; a sensitivity sweep — not an exhaustive audit — tells you which. That triages the infinite-verification problem.

2. The hardest case in the cascade

Most load-bearing conclusions never touch these numbers. The area-conservation identity (M45: dAopen + dAclose = 0) is purely geometric; the dual-redox result (M42) is a sign / direction argument. Neither uses density or thickness, so neither can be contaminated by them. The one place theory-laden geophysical constants do flow into a present-tense conclusion is M35’s forebulge-position test, which predicts the peripheral bulge offset from the lithospheric flexural wavelength:

D = E·Te³/(12(1−ν²)),   α = (4D/(ρmg))1/4,   band = [0.5πα, 1.0πα]; tested against observed offsets (US Mid-Atlantic 300 km, North Sea 250 km). Since α ∝ E1/4·Te3/4·ρm−1/4·(1−ν²)−1/4, the elastic thickness dominates.

3. The sweep (pre-registered contamination ranges; no tuning)

The four theory-laden inputs are swept across their full literature spread — Te [40,100] km, E [50,100] GPa, ρm [3200,3400],  ν [0.20,0.30] — over a 315-point grid including every worst-case corner, asking whether both observed offsets stay bracketed at every point.

4. Verdict — ROBUST

Across the full contamination hypercube — all four un-verifiable inputs simultaneously at every grid point, worst-case corners included — both observed forebulge offsets stay bracketed. The conclusion does not depend on the precise values of Te / E / ρm / ν; it would break only if the elastic thickness fell below ~35 km or rose above ~160 km, outside the literature range for the cratonic lithosphere these forebulges sit on. So here the theory-laden inputs are not load-bearing, and auditing them to the bottom is unnecessary. Had the conclusion flipped inside the ranges, the sweep would have flagged the dependency rather than hidden it — that is the method.

5. Honest scope

This converts a philosophical worry into a verified robustness statement, but it claims exactly what it shows and no more. M35 is itself non-discriminating (elasticity is shared by both frameworks), so this is a method demonstration: even a theory-laden [V] claim is checked for input-dependence rather than trusted. The genuine residual remains honest: if some input were ever both load-bearing and irreducibly mainstream-corrected, independence could not be claimed there — the honest move would be to state the conditional dependency, not bury it. And “frozen” never meant “true”: a SHA-pin asserts the input is disclosed and replaceable, delegating verification to the reader, who can substitute their own value and re-derive. Occurrence and absolute ages stay [O], both directions.
