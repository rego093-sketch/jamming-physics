Module 33 — Latitude energy-balance permission screen: the termination fork

In plain terms. The Sun does not warm the planet the same way through the year. Averaged over a year the equator gets the most light; at midsummer the pole actually gets the most (24-hour day). So when an ice age ends, which latitude melts first depends on which of those two balances controls the melt — and that is a fork in the road, not one fixed story. What we can check today is firm: the ice that exists at high latitudes is locked in by its own brightness, exactly as a bistable switch would predict.
