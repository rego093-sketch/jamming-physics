Module 37 — Source-rock facies census: non-discriminating on rate (a prior refutation retracted)

In plain terms. The rocks that source the world's giant oil fields are finely layered marine shales — the signature of quiet, oxygen-starved bottom water. That rules out a violent, chaotic flood deposit. But fine layering does not mean “slow”: rapid burial of organic matter in a stratified, oxygen-free ocean also makes fine layers. So the facies cannot decide fast-vs-slow. An earlier version treated “laminated” as “slow” and called the rapid origin refuted; that was a rate claim the firewall forbids, and it is withdrawn.

1. The present-tense facies

From repro/data/source_rock_facies.csv: every giant-field source rock is finely laminated, intra-fill, Type II (or II-S) kerogen, several–tens % TOC — the signature of a quiet, stratified, anoxic basin. 0/7 are basal-chaotic / ice-rafted-debris deposits. [V].

2. What it excludes, and what it does not

It excludes a high-energy chaotic deposit — but the cascade's rapid origin was never a chaotic diamictite; it is rapid organic burial under a meltwater-lid stratified anoxic ocean, which also makes laminae. “Laminated” is not “slow”. So the facies are non-discriminating on rate, with the discriminator in the firewalled timing.
