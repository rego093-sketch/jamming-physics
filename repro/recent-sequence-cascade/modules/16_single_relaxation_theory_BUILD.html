Module 16 — The Single-Relaxation Theory of the Coupled Cascade (BUILD mode)

Thesis, sharpened. The five "events" (flood, glaciation, opening, termination, petroleum) are not five claims and not a sequence of tuned coincidences. They are the single switching transition of one bistable substrate between two stable states — and the entire transition is controlled by one parameter: the mass of jammed-water reservoir that releases. Develop that, and a chain of distinctive, theory-internal predictions follows.

1. The theory in one structure: one substrate, two states, one switch, one parameter

The VP corpus already supplies a universal bistable substrate — the R19 switch, ṡ = g·s − s³ + h, with two stable states separated by an unstable threshold. The Recent-Sequence cascade is that switch, realised in planetary water-and-crust:

  • State A — "loaded cold greenhouse." Water and heat are held in a high-pressure, solid-like configuration: jammed State-4 water (ice-VII), which the deep Earth demonstrably stores (Module 12). This is a metastable well — a loaded spring. It is stable until perturbed past threshold.
  • State B — "unloaded new equilibrium." Water released to the surface, heat dissipated, the crust reconfigured, the spring spent. A second stable well.
  • The switch (h crosses threshold). A shock — impact, internal stress, or glacial loading — pushes the system over the unstable ridge. The transition is not gradual drift; it is a relaxation down the potential, fast at first (steep gradient) and self-tapering as it approaches the new well.

The single controlling parameter. The amplitude of everything that follows is set by how much reservoir releases — call it the release mass. One number, fixed at the switch, sets the flood volume, the ocean warming, the ice built, the loading that triggers rupture, and the heat that closes the loop. This is the theory's defining economy: a one-parameter relaxation, not a five-parameter coincidence.

2. The engine: why one destabilisation does three things at once

The power of the substrate is that a single jammed-water destabilisation has three simultaneous mechanical consequences — it does not cause three later events, it is all three:

  1. Water out → Flood. Losing confining pressure melts the high-pressure ice (depressurisation of ice-VII → liquid, present-tense observable). The water floods out. Total water conserved; only its state changes (State 4 → liquid).
  2. Energy out → Warm ocean. The release dissipates the loaded spring's stored energy — gravitational (a vast water mass redistributing and dropping in level) plus the configurational energy of the unjamming transition — as heat in the released water. The ocean is left warm, under a dust-laden cold sky. This pairing is not a separate event; it is the same release seen thermally.
  3. Support gone → Rupture. The rigid State-4 enclosure that was holding a void open collapses when its connectivity is lost (it cannot pass through the forbidden intermediate state, so it fails geometrically), opening a void that the inherited void-suction / unjamming engine drives into a rupture.

So flood, warm-ocean fuel, and rupture trigger are one event read three ways. The cascade then chains these into glaciation and termination. The theory's job from here is to develop the hand-offs and extract the distinctive predictions.

3. The coupled chain as one connected system (developing the hand-offs)

Each stage manufactures the next stage's initial conditions — this is the structure a fragmented account cannot have. Developed as a connected system:

                 ├─▶ OCEAN warming ΔT ──▶ snow flux (C-C × ΔT) ──▶ ICE mass built
                 │                                                     │
                 │                                            ICE loads crust
                 │                                                     ▼

                                                                       │

The warm ocean (consequence 2) is the fuel line that feeds the ice engine; the ice mass is the loading line that feeds the rupture; the rupture heat is the melt line that closes the loop. One reservoir release lights all three lines. The chain is self-supporting if the hand-offs balance — and balance is a prediction the theory makes, not an assumption (see §4, P3).

4. Distinctive predictions derived from the theory's own logic

These follow from the one-substrate, one-relaxation structure and differ from a fragmented / slow-cyclic account. They are stated as what the theory predicts — the targets a later stress-test will aim at.

P1 — Coupling co-location (flood ↔ gateway/rifting ↔ glaciation are linked, not independent)

Because one destabilisation drives all stages, the theory predicts their signatures are causally and spatially coupled, not randomly co-distributed. Specifically: a major basin flood/desiccation should be tied to an associated gateway or rifting event, and rapid glaciation should be tied to an associated ocean-warming source. The theory therefore predicts that "a flood whose control knob is a connection/rifting event" is a recurring structure — not a one-off. (One realised instance already exists: a basin-scale flood gated by an oceanic gateway, Module 12.)

P2 — A single asymmetric pulse (the signature waveform)

Because each stage self-limits — the ocean gives up its heat so the snow engine tapers; the rupture re-jams on a short self-limit — the theory predicts the whole relaxation has a characteristic shape: rapid onset, self-tapering, single pulse, reaching a new equilibrium. This is distinct from a train of evenly-spaced cycles. The theory predicts the record should read as one asymmetric relaxation (sharp start, long taper), with the build phase limited by ocean-heat supply and the termination phase faster (P3).

P3 — Energy closure with a build/melt asymmetry (a quantitative structural prediction)

The same reservoir funds both building and melting the ice — and the theory predicts how they relate. Building ice goes through evaporation (the ocean pays the latent heat of vaporisation); melting it back pays only the latent heat of fusion. Since vaporisation costs several times more than fusion, the theory predicts a built-in asymmetry: initiation is the expensive, ocean-heat-limited phase; termination is energetically cheap once the rupture restores warm circulation. Distinctive consequence: the loop closes with slow-ish build, fast melt — a rapid termination is the theory's natural prediction, not a puzzle. (This also predicts the ice built ≈ a fixed function of the ocean warming, so the magnitudes are linked, feeding P5.)

P4 — Material co-occurrence (one engine, a specific mineral/rock suite)

One jammed-water release leaves a specific co-occurring suite: evaporites (the flood water's dissolved load concentrating as the system re-equilibrates), organic-rich fine source rock (the flood/glacial fine sediment settling in the new low-energy basins), and rift/volcanic products (from the rupture). The theory therefore predicts these should be found together, in causal association, not as unrelated accidents. (A realised co-occurrence: a basin with a kilometre-scale salt giant, organic-rich shales, and an oceanic-gateway rifting control — Module 12.)

P5 — One-parameter scaling (correlated magnitudes)

Because one number (release mass) sets every amplitude, the theory predicts the magnitudes correlate across instances: a larger release → a larger flood and a larger ice build and a larger rupture, scaling together. The theory predicts flood magnitude, glaciation extent, and opening rate are not independent knobs but one knob seen in three places — so they should co-vary. This is the sharpest distinguishing prediction from any account that treats the three as separate tunable events.

5. The positive consilience case (what one engine buys)

A fragmented account needs separate, independently-tuned mechanisms for: a great flood, a rapid glaciation, an accelerated ocean opening, a rapid deglaciation, and the formation of giant petroleum + salt provinces. The Single-Relaxation theory buys all of them from one substrate switch and one parameter:

  • the flood and the opening are the same destabilisation (water-out and void-collapse) — not two events needing a coincidence in timing;
  • the glaciation is the thermal face of the flood (warm ocean + cold sky), so it does not need an independent orbital trigger to start fast;
  • the termination is the rupture's heat closing the loop — not a separate warming event;
  • the petroleum + salt + rift suite is the material residue of the single release (P4).

This is the theory's distinctive virtue: consilience from economy. Five phenomena that otherwise require five mechanisms and a fortuitous ordering become one relaxation of one loaded substrate, with the ordering forced by the hand-offs (each stage makes the next). A theory that explains a suite from one engine, with the order built in, has a structure that a collection of separate mechanisms cannot claim.
