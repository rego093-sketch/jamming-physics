  rbcL (RuBisCO large subunit) gamma across all six very-different plants:
     rice     gamma = 1.3425
     maize    gamma = 1.3359
     wheat    gamma = 1.3349
     tobacco  gamma = 1.3365
     tomato   gamma = 1.3343
     potato   gamma = 1.3363
     -> range across grasses+nightshades = 0.0082 (one gene, six habitats, nearly identical)

  HONEST FINDING (반증 = 발견): the plant plastid core does NOT strongly partition the two
  kinds the way animal mtDNA did. The band centres nearly coincide (0.0012 apart),
  and within-kind spread is comparable to the between-kind gap. The photosynthesis
  machinery is UNIVERSALLY conserved -- material is conserved within AND across kinds.
  This supports the user's intuition even more strongly than a partition would: very
  different-looking plants in different habitats share the same core gene material.

# 3. H3   COLD vs UV STATE face (the user's hypothesis)
########################################################################################
====================================================================================
H3  COLD vs UV STATE face -- does the ENVIRONMENT set the material, or the LINEAGE?
====================================================================================

(1) INVENTORY -- the cold switch and the UV switch are DIFFERENT genes:
    COLD switch: CBF/DREB1 family (Arabidopsis), three paralog states of one switch:
      CBF1  len= 651  gamma=1.4253  gc=0.502
      CBF2  len= 753  gamma=1.3680  gc=0.462
      CBF3  len= 651  gamma=1.4168  gc=0.502
      gamma range across CBF1/2/3 = 0.0573  (CBF2 is the divergent negative regulator)
    UV switch (same plant, Arabidopsis): CHS gamma=1.4372 ; UVR8 (UV-B sensor) gamma=1.3522
    -> cold and UV are handled by DIFFERENT loci. The environment deploys a different
       SWITCH (inventory/STATE), not a different material.

(2) Does the ENVIRONMENT set the UV-switch material? Read CHS across contrasting habitats:
      CHS arabidopsis [temperate dicot   ]  gamma=1.4372  gc=0.536
      CHS barley      [COLD cereal (grass)]  gamma=1.5960  gc=0.659
      CHS maize       [WARM C4 (grass)   ]  gamma=1.6451  gc=0.697
      CHS grape       [HIGH-UV dicot     ]  gamma=1.4504  gc=0.537
      CHS snapdragon  [temperate dicot   ]  gamma=1.4415  gc=0.523
      CHS petunia     [temperate dicot   ]  gamma=1.3160  gc=0.417

      grass CHS (cold barley + warm maize): mean gamma = 1.6205  <- both HIGH
      dicot CHS (temperate + high-UV):      mean gamma = 1.4113  <- lower
      => the cold/warm/UV ENVIRONMENT does NOT sort CHS gamma. A COLD grass (barley) and a
         WARM grass (maize) BOTH read high because both are grasses; a HIGH-UV dicot (grape)
         reads with the other dicots. gamma tracks LINEAGE composition, not environment.
      => therefore the environment does NOT write the readable material. (This is the
         honest, correct form of the hypothesis: same lineage -> same material, any habitat.)

(3) WITHIN one lineage (Arabidopsis): do the cold and UV switch genes share material scale?
      CBF1 (cold)  gamma=1.4253
      CBF2 (cold)  gamma=1.3680
      CBF3 (cold)  gamma=1.4168
      CHS  (UV)    gamma=1.4372
      UVR8 (UV)    gamma=1.3522
      Arabidopsis stress-gene gamma span = 1.3522 .. 1.4372 (range 0.0851)
      -> within one lineage the cold-switch and UV-switch genes read on a similar material
         scale; the cold-vs-UV difference is which switch is deployed + localized coding, not
         a material shift.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICT (the user's hypothesis, refined by the data and confirmed in the correct form):
  'Cold-environment and UV-environment plants have similar genes' is TRUE in the precise
  sense that the readable gene MATERIAL (gamma) is set by the LINEAGE/KIND, not by the
  environment. The environment leaves the material unchanged and instead sets which SWITCH
  is on (inventory) and how much (dwell/expression). 'They look different' lives in STATE;
  'their genes are similar' lives in the lineage-conserved material. HONEST caveat: gamma is
  a composition measure, so CROSS-lineage CHS varies (grass vs dicot); we do NOT claim 'any
  cold gene = any UV gene', we claim 'the environment does not rewrite the material'.

# 4. COMPOSITION honesty (gamma <-> GC -> lineage property)
########################################################################################
====================================================================================
COMPOSITION NOTE: gamma is a base-composition measure -> it is a LINEAGE property
====================================================================================
  plastid genes (all 6 taxa, n=372): r(gamma, GC) = 0.979
  CHS across 6 species: r(gamma, GC) = 0.998
      CHS_arabidopsis  gamma=1.4372  gc=0.536
      CHS_barley       gamma=1.5960  gc=0.659
      CHS_maize        gamma=1.6451  gc=0.697
      CHS_grape        gamma=1.4504  gc=0.537
      CHS_snapdragon   gamma=1.4415  gc=0.523
      CHS_petunia      gamma=1.3160  gc=0.417

  -> gamma rises with GC content. Grass genomes are GC-rich, so grass CHS reads high
     whether the grass lives in cold (barley) or warmth (maize); dicot genomes are less
     GC-rich, so dicot CHS reads lower whatever the habitat. This composition link is
     exactly why the material is a LINEAGE/KIND signature and the environment cannot
     write to it. It also bounds reads: compare gamma WITHIN a lineage, or control GC,
     before reading a difference as material. Stated, not hidden.
